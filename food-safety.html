🧪 Commonly Adulterated Foods in India

These everyday foods are frequently adulterated. Learn what adulterants are used, the associated health risks, and simple indicative home checks — always follow up with certified laboratory testing.

🔴 HIGH SEVERITY

🥛 Milk

Common Adulterants

  • Water
  • Detergent / Soap
  • Starch
  • Urea
  • Synthetic milk
  • Formalin

Health Risks

Detergent causes gastroenteritis and kidney damage. Urea is toxic to the liver and kidneys. Formalin is a known carcinogen. Synthetic milk can cause metabolic disorders.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Detergent: Shake a small amount in a bottle — excessive persistent foam indicates detergent. Starch: Add a few drops of iodine solution; blue-black colour suggests starch. Water: A drop of milk on a polished surface slides slowly if pure, quickly if watered down.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🔴 HIGH SEVERITY

🌿 Turmeric Powder

Common Adulterants

  • Lead chromate
  • Chalk powder
  • Metanil yellow dye
  • Artificial colour

Health Risks

Lead chromate is highly toxic, causing neurological damage, kidney failure, and anaemia. Metanil yellow is a non-permitted dye linked to cancer and nervous system disorders.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Chalk/starch: Add a teaspoon to a glass of water — chalk settles quickly. Metanil yellow: Add a few drops of HCl (if safely available) — a pink colour indicates the dye. Lead chromate: Add a few drops of concentrated HCl to the sample — it turns a pink/magenta colour.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🔴 HIGH SEVERITY

🌶️ Chilli Powder

Common Adulterants

  • Brick dust
  • Sawdust
  • Sudan Red dye
  • Artificial colour
  • Starch

Health Risks

Sudan Red is a probable human carcinogen (IARC Group 3). Brick dust and sawdust can damage the digestive tract and cause intestinal injuries over time.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Brick dust: Mix with water and let settle — brick dust particles will sink, and water may turn red/orange. Sawdust: Place on water surface — sawdust floats while genuine chilli powder partially dissolves.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY

🍯 Honey

Common Adulterants

  • Sugar syrup
  • High-fructose corn syrup
  • Jaggery / glucose
  • Artificial colouring

Health Risks

Adulterated honey provides no genuine health benefits, causes blood sugar spikes — particularly dangerous for diabetics — and defeats the antimicrobial properties of real honey.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Thumb test: Place a drop on your thumb — pure honey stays in place, adulterated honey spreads. Water test: Add a spoonful to water — pure honey settles at the bottom without dissolving immediately. Paper test: A drop on blotting paper — adulterated honey leaves a wet mark.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🔴 HIGH SEVERITY

🌱 Mustard Seeds

Common Adulterants

  • Argemone seeds (toxic)
  • Grass seeds

Health Risks

Epidemic dropsy: Argemone seeds contain sanguinarine and dihydrosanguinarine, which cause severe oedema (swelling), glaucoma, diarrhoea, and can be fatal in high doses. This is a documented public health crisis in India.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Visual check: Argemone seeds are slightly larger, rough-surfaced, and dark brownish-black with a rough coat, unlike smooth yellowish or dark brown mustard seeds. Crush test: Mustard seeds are yellow inside; argemone seeds are white inside.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY

🫙 Edible Oils

Common Adulterants

  • Mineral oil
  • Cheaper vegetable oils
  • Palm oil
  • Rancid oils

Health Risks

Mineral oil is non-digestible and can accumulate in organs. Blended cheaper oils alter the fatty acid profile, negating health claims. Rancid oils produce free radicals linked to cancer and cardiovascular disease.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Cold test for coconut oil: Genuine coconut oil solidifies at 24°C; adulterated oil may remain liquid. Rancidity smell: Sniff carefully — rancid oils have a bitter, off-putting odour. Note: definitive adulteration tests require lab equipment.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY

🍵 Tea Leaves

Common Adulterants

  • Exhausted (used) tea
  • Iron filings
  • Artificial colouring
  • Sawdust

Health Risks

Artificial dyes may be non-permitted and carcinogenic. Iron filings cause digestive issues. Exhausted tea has no nutritional value. Consumed regularly, these adulterants burden the liver and kidneys.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Iron filings: Pass a magnet through a thin spread of dry tea — iron filings will be attracted. Artificial colour: Soak a blotting paper in tea and let it dry — artificial dye leaves a distinct coloured stain rather than the natural brownish ring of real tea.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.
🟠 MEDIUM SEVERITY

🧀 Paneer / Khoya

Common Adulterants

  • Starch
  • Vanaspati (hydrogenated fat)
  • Soda
  • Skimmed milk powder
  • Synthetic milk solids

Health Risks

Trans fats from vanaspati increase cardiovascular disease risk. Starch adulteration reduces protein quality. Synthetic dairy solids often contain harmful chemicals that affect kidney and liver function.

🔬 Indicative Home Test

Starch in paneer: Add a few drops of iodine solution to a small piece — a blue-black colour indicates starch adulteration. Vanaspati: Dissolve a small piece in hot water, add a few drops of iodine — pure paneer turns light brown; vanaspati-adulterated paneer reacts differently.

⚠️ These tests are indicative only — not conclusive proof.

👁️ Warning Signs to Watch For

Before testing, your senses and sharp observation are your first line of defence. Be alert to these red flags when buying or using food products.

👃

Unusual Smell or Off-Odour

Any sour, chemical, musty, or otherwise unexpected smell in a food product is a serious warning. Trust your nose — if it doesn't smell right, don't consume it.

🎨

Abnormal Colour

Unnaturally bright, too dull, or unevenly distributed colour can signal added synthetic dyes or other adulterants. Compare with authentic product colour you know.

✋

Unexpected Texture or Consistency

Grittiness in spices, unusual viscosity in oils or honey, or a grainy feel in dairy products can indicate foreign matter mixed in to bulk up the product.

🔍

Sediment or Foreign Matter Visible

Visible particles that shouldn't be there — specks in oil, lumps in spice powder, or floating debris — are a clear warning sign of contamination or adulteration.

💸

Price Too Cheap for Quality Claimed

If the price is significantly lower than market rate for a premium product, be sceptical. Adulterants are often added specifically to cut production costs and sell at "bargain" prices.

📦

Packaging Damage or Broken Seals

Torn, resealed, punctured, or otherwise tampered packaging indicates that the product may have been adulterated after leaving the original manufacturer. Always check seals.

🏷️

Missing or Illegible FSSAI Licence Number

Every legally sold food product in India must carry a valid FSSAI licence number. Its absence, blurring, or suspicious formatting is a major red flag requiring immediate action.

📅

Tampered or Suspicious Best-Before Date

Smudged, over-printed, stickered-over, or inconsistent best-before or manufacturing dates indicate potential label fraud or date-tampering on expired products.

🚨 What To Do If You Suspect Adulteration

Follow these steps carefully if you believe a food product may be adulterated. Your report could protect many other consumers.

  1. 1

    Stop Using the Product Immediately

    Cease all consumption or use of the suspected product right away. Do not serve it to others, including children or elderly family members who may be more vulnerable.

  2. 2

    Do NOT Taste or Sniff Directly to Test

    Resist the urge to taste or inhale closely to detect adulterants. Some substances (like argemone alkaloids or lead compounds) are toxic even in tiny quantities. Never assume a small amount is safe.

  3. 3

    Note All Label Details

    Carefully write down or photograph: the batch number, manufacturing date, best-before date, FSSAI licence number, barcode/QR code, and the full address of the manufacturer. This is critical for any official report.

  4. 4

    Photograph the Packaging and Product

    Take clear, well-lit photographs of the full packaging (all sides), any visible damage or tampering, and — if safely possible — the product itself. These photos serve as evidence for your complaint.

  5. 5

    Contact the Brand's Consumer Helpline

    Many large food companies have consumer helplines or grievance cells. Reporting directly may trigger a faster product recall. The number is usually printed on the packaging.

  6. 6

    Report Online at FSSAI Food Safety Connect Portal

    File a formal complaint at foodsafety.gov.in. This is the official government portal for food safety complaints. You can upload your photos and provide all product details.

  7. 7

    Or Call FSSAI Helpline: 1800-112-100 (Toll-Free)

    You can call the FSSAI toll-free helpline 1800-112-100 to report food safety concerns verbally. The helpline is operational during government working hours and can guide you through the complaint process.

  8. 8

    Seek Laboratory Testing If Needed

    For definitive confirmation, contact an NABL-accredited food testing laboratory. FSSAI maintains a list of approved labs. Laboratory results are required for any legal proceedings. Keep the original product sample sealed and labelled.
